# Share Management & Cap Table Solutions Guide

**Choosing the right platform for equity management, cap tables, and compliance**

---

## Introduction

Managing equity, cap tables, and ownership in your startup is critical for legal compliance, fundraising, and team transparency. As soon as you incorporate and issue shares (even to yourself), you need proper equity tracking.

This guide helps you choose between cap table management solutions based on your stage, complexity, and budget.

---

## What is a Cap Table?

A **capitalization table (cap table)** is a spreadsheet or document that shows:
- Who owns what percentage of your company
- Different share classes (common, preferred, options)
- Vesting schedules
- Dilution over time
- Convertible instruments (SAFEs, convertible notes)

### Why Cap Tables Matter:
- **Legal compliance**: Required for tax filings and corporate governance
- **Fundraising**: Investors need to see ownership structure
- **409A valuations**: Required for option grants (US)
- **Employee equity**: Track option pools and grants
- **Exit planning**: Calculate proceeds distribution
- **Transparency**: Keep everyone aligned on ownership

---

## When You Need Cap Table Management

### You NEED Cap Table Management:
- **Day 1**: As soon as you incorporate
- **Issuing equity** to founders, employees, or investors
- **Fundraising**: Before talking to investors
- **Employee stock options**: Need proper tracking and compliance
- **Multiple share classes**: Common + Preferred stock
- **Convertible instruments**: SAFEs, convertible notes
- **409A valuation** required (US companies)
- **More than 10 stakeholders**

### You Don't Need Dedicated Platform (yet):
- **Solo founder, no plans to raise or hire**: Simple spreadsheet works
- **Very early stage** (pre-incorporation): No company = no cap table

**Reality Check**: If you've incorporated, you should have a cap table. The only question is: spreadsheet or software?

## Decision Framework

### Key Questions

1. **What's your funding stage?**
   - Bootstrapped, no fundraising plans → Spreadsheet or free tier
   - Pre-seed / raising first round → Carta, Pulley, or AngelList
   - Seed+ with investors → Dedicated platform (Carta, Pulley)
   - Series A+ → Professional platform (Carta)

2. **How many stakeholders?**
   - 1-5 (founders only) → Spreadsheet or free tier
   - 6-25 (founders + early employees) → Pulley or AngelList
   - 25-100 → Carta or Pulley
   - 100+ → Carta

3. **Do you need 409A valuations?**
   - Yes (US company granting options) → Platform with 409A (Carta, Pulley)
   - No (non-US or not granting options) → More flexibility

4. **What's your budget?**
   - $0 → Spreadsheet or AngelList
   - $100-500/year → Pulley, AngelList
   - $500-3000/year → Pulley
   - $3000+/year → Carta

5. **Complexity?**
   - Simple (common stock only) → Spreadsheet
   - Moderate (options, SAFEs) → Pulley, AngelList
   - Complex (multiple rounds, preferences) → Carta

## Implementation Best Practices

### 1. Set Up From Day 1
- Record incorporation details immediately
- Document founder equity allocation
- Track all equity issuances
- Keep records of board approvals

### 2. Maintain Accuracy
- Update after every equity event
- Record option exercises immediately
- Track vesting milestones
- Regular audits (quarterly)

### 3. Stakeholder Communication
- Give employees access to their equity info
- Provide investor portal access
- Regular updates to board
- Transparency builds trust

### 4. Compliance
- Keep corporate resolutions
- File necessary tax forms (83(b) elections, etc.)
- Annual 409A valuations (if granting options)
- Board approval for equity grants

### 5. Planning
- Model dilution before fundraising
- Understand option pool dynamics
- Plan for future rounds
- Exit scenario modeling

## Critical Cap Table Concepts

### Founder Vesting
- **Why**: Protects company if founder leaves early
- **Standard**: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff
- **Implementation**: Even founders should have vesting

### Option Pool
- **What**: Reserved shares for employee grants
- **Typical Size**: 10-20% pre-money
- **Refreshes**: Increased in funding rounds
- **Dilution**: Founders diluted to create pool

### 409A Valuations
- **What**: IRS-required valuation of common stock
- **When**: Before first option grants, then annually
- **Cost**: $1,000-5,000 standalone, free with Carta/Pulley
- **US Only**: Not required outside US

### Dilution
- **What**: Ownership percentage decrease when new shares issued
- **Manage**: Understand dilution before each round
- **Model**: Use platform scenario tools

## Red Flags to Avoid

### Don't:
- ❌ Use spreadsheet long-term (error-prone, unprofessional)
- ❌ Grant options without 409A (IRS issues)
- ❌ Ignore cap table maintenance (becomes mess quickly)
- ❌ Hide equity info from employees (creates mistrust)
- ❌ Make verbal equity promises (must be documented)

### Do:
- ✅ Set up proper platform when you incorporate
- ✅ Get 409A before granting options
- ✅ Keep cap table updated
- ✅ Give stakeholders transparency
- ✅ Model scenarios before fundraising

## Migration Path

### Phase 1: Incorporation
- Use lawyer's template or AngelList free tier
- Record initial founder shares

### Phase 2: First Equity Events (hiring, fundraising)
- Migrate to Pulley or Carta
- Get 409A valuation
- Proper equity grant documentation

### Phase 3: Growth (Series A+)
- Consider Carta if not already using
- Professional investor relations
- Complex modeling

## Common Mistakes

1. **Waiting too long** to set up proper cap table
2. **Not vesting founder shares** (causes issues if founder leaves)
3. **Granting options without 409A** (tax problems)
4. **Inconsistent updates** (cap table becomes unreliable)
5. **Hiding equity from employees** (creates mistrust)
6. **Verbal equity promises** (not legally binding)
7. **Ignoring dilution modeling** (surprised by ownership after rounds)
